Traditional Georgian Food

Khinkali

Khinkali are large Georgian dumplings filled with meat, herbs, onions, and flavorful broth. Some versions are made with cheese, mushrooms, or potatoes. They are traditionally eaten by hand. People first take a small bite, drink the hot broth inside, and then eat the rest of the dumpling, leaving only the thick top knot on the plate.

Khachapuri

Khachapuri is the most famous Georgian dish. It is a traditional bread filled with cheese, and there are many regional varieties. The best-known version is Adjarian khachapuri, which is shaped like a boat and topped with a raw egg and butter. It is usually eaten while it is still hot, by mixing the cheese, egg, and butter together before tearing off pieces of the bread.

Churchkhela

Churchkhela is a traditional Georgian sweet often called the "Georgian candy." It is made by threading walnuts or hazelnuts onto a string and dipping them several times into thickened grape juice. After drying, it forms a chewy, sweet coating. Churchkhela is a popular snack and can be stored for a long time.

Mtsvadi

Mtsvadi is the Georgian version of barbecue. Large pieces of pork, beef, or lamb are grilled over an open fire until they are juicy and tender. It is usually served with fresh onions, herbs, vegetables, and tkemali, a traditional sour plum sauce. Mtsvadi is especially popular during family gatherings and outdoor celebrations.

Lobio

Lobio is a traditional bean dish made with red kidney beans, onions, garlic, herbs, and spices. It is often cooked in a clay pot and served with cornbread called mchadi and pickled vegetables. Lobio is simple, healthy, and one of the most popular vegetarian dishes in Georgia.

Kharcho

Kharcho is a rich and flavorful Georgian soup traditionally made with beef, rice, tomatoes, walnuts, garlic, and aromatic herbs and spices. It has a slightly spicy and tangy taste and is often enjoyed with fresh Georgian bread. Kharcho is considered one of the country's most comforting and traditional dishes.
