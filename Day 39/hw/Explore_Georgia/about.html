About Georgia

Georgia is a country located at the crossroads of Eastern Europe and Western Asia in the Caucasus region. It has a long and fascinating history that stretches back thousands of years. People have lived in this area since prehistoric times, and Georgia is considered one of the oldest continuously inhabited regions in the world. The first Georgian kingdoms, Colchis and Iberia, were established during ancient times. These kingdoms played an important role in trade and culture between Europe and Asia.

The modern Georgian nation developed over many centuries. In the 4th century AD, Georgia became one of the first countries in the world to adopt Christianity as its official religion, in the year 337. This event had a lasting influence on the country's culture, traditions, and architecture. Throughout its history, Georgia experienced periods of independence as well as invasions by different empires. In 1918, Georgia declared its independence as the Democratic Republic of Georgia, but in 1921 it became part of the Soviet Union. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, Georgia regained its independence on April 9, 1991.

The capital city of Georgia is Tbilisi, which is also the country's largest city. According to legend, Tbilisi was founded in the 5th century by King Vakhtang I Gorgasali after he discovered warm sulfur springs while hunting. The name "Tbilisi" comes from the Georgian word "tbili," meaning "warm," referring to these natural hot springs. Today, the sulfur baths are still one of the city's most famous attractions.

Tbilisi is a city where ancient history meets modern life. Its Old Town is filled with narrow streets, colorful wooden balconies, historic churches, mosques, synagogues, and traditional houses. The city reflects the many cultures and civilizations that have influenced Georgia over the centuries. Famous landmarks include Narikala Fortress, which overlooks the city, the Peace Bridge, the Holy Trinity Cathedral, and Mtatsminda Park, which offers beautiful views of the capital.

Georgia is also famous for its hospitality, delicious food, and beautiful landscapes. Traditional dishes such as khachapuri, khinkali, and churchkhela are enjoyed by both locals and visitors. The country is recognized as the birthplace of wine, with a winemaking tradition that is more than 8,000 years old. From the snowy peaks of the Greater Caucasus Mountains to the beaches of the Black Sea, Georgia offers a rich mix of nature, history, and culture, making it a unique and memorable country.
